How Much Does a Hair Topper Cost in the UK?

By Tatiana Karelina, hair topper and wig specialist · Published 12 May 2026

In the UK, hair toppers range from under £100 for mass produced synthetic pieces to £2,000 or more for fully bespoke human hair. At Tatiana Karelina Toppers and Wigs, guide prices for a made to measure Russian hair topper are £1,050 to £1,900 for a classic, £615 to £1,335 for a frontal and £1,100 to £1,950 for a halo, with wiglets priced individually. Price is driven by 4 things: the hair itself, Russian hair being the most prized for softness and natural lustre, the base type, since silk costs more than mono because of its hand finished parting, the length and weight of hair, and the construction time, with every piece handcrafted over 8 to 12 weeks. A 50% deposit at time of order confirms your piece and the balance is settled on completion.

Why do prices vary so widely?

A £90 topper and a £1,500 topper are different products that happen to share a name. Mass produced pieces use processed, silicone coated hair on standard bases in standard colours; the shine washes out, the fibre tangles, and the colour never quite matches. A bespoke piece is built from unprocessed Russian hair on a base made to your measurements, in a colour blended by hand from several ponytails without dye. One is bought; the other is made for you.

What exactly are you paying for?

The hair is the largest cost: Russian hair is sourced at source through a network of trusted collectors before brokers blend it with cheaper Asian hair. Then the base, mono for breathability, silk for the most realistic parting, hybrid for both. Then hours of skilled construction, colour blending, base building, quality checks. And around the piece itself: a free consultation, fitting, and aftercare guidance so the topper lasts 2 years or longer.

How payment works

A 50% deposit at time of order confirms your piece; the balance is settled on completion, 8 to 12 weeks later, when you are fitted in London or Manchester or your piece is shipped ready to wear. Some women start there, and most find the colour mismatch and artificial shine make them unwearable in daylight. If budget is the constraint, a smaller bespoke wiglet often serves better than a large synthetic piece.
