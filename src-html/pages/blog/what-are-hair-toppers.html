What Are Hair Toppers and Who Are They For?

By Tatiana Karelina, hair topper and wig specialist · Published 19 May 2026

A hair topper is a lightweight hairpiece that covers a specific area of thinning or hair loss rather than the whole head. It blends with your natural hair, adding volume and coverage exactly where it is needed, which makes it lighter, cooler and easier to wear daily than a full wig. Toppers suit women with partial loss: thinning at the crown or parting, recession at the hairline or temples, or more advanced thinning where there is still hair to blend with. Tatiana Karelina Toppers and Wigs is the only company in the UK offering all 3 custom topper types, classic, frontal and halo, each made to measure from Russian hair and colour matched without dye. A bespoke topper takes 8 to 12 weeks to handcraft and is fitted in our London or Manchester salons, or arranged entirely by video, worldwide.

How is a topper different from a wig?

A wig replaces all of your hair; a topper supplements it. Because a topper covers only the area that needs help, it weighs a fraction of a wig, lets your scalp breathe, and uses your own hairline and side hair, which is why a good topper is so hard to detect. The trade is that a topper needs enough of your own hair to anchor and blend with, which is why the consultation starts with an honest look at your pattern of loss.

Who suits which type?

The classic covers general thinning at the crown and parting, the everyday choice for most clients. The frontal rebuilds the front hairline and temples with a hand ventilated lace edge, ideal for recession and frontal fibrosing alopecia. The halo suits more advanced thinning, holding with a gentle adjustable band instead of clips, so it asks nothing of fragile hair. Wiglets, the smallest pieces, add targeted volume at the crown or parting.
