Postpartum Hair Loss and Toppers: What to Expect

By Tatiana Karelina, hair topper and wig specialist · Published 16 June 2026

Postpartum hair loss is common and usually temporary. During pregnancy, high oestrogen holds hairs in their growth phase; after birth, those retained hairs shed together, often 3 to 4 months postpartum, and recovery typically completes within a year. The difficulty is living through the in between, when the parting widens and the hairline breaks into short regrowth. Why does hair fall out after pregnancy?

During pregnancy, hormones hold more hairs than usual in the growth phase, which is why hair often feels thicker. After birth, hormone levels reset and the held hairs release together, a shed called telogen effluvium. It can look alarming on the pillow and in the shower, but it is the cycle correcting itself, and for most women density recovers within 12 months.

How does a topper help in the meantime?

A topper or wiglet gives back the density while your own hair recovers underneath. When does postpartum shedding stop?+

It usually peaks 3 to 4 months after birth and settles within 6 to 12 months. If shedding continues beyond a year or comes with other symptoms, see your GP.

Topper or wiglet for postpartum loss?+

Usually a wiglet, the smallest piece, because postpartum loss is diffuse and temporary. If thinning is wider or was present before pregnancy, a classic topper gives more coverage.
