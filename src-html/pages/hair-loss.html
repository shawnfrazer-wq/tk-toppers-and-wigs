Causes

The causes of female hair loss

Genetic and hormonal thinning

Hair loss can affect any woman at any age, often beginning subtly with fine thinning at the crown or parting. Female pattern hair loss is genetic and progresses slowly over months or years. Hormonal shifts from pregnancy, menopause or PCOS can spark diffuse shedding, while stress or sudden weight loss can send healthy hairs into a resting phase.

Autoimmune conditions such as alopecia areata attack individual follicles and cause patchy bald spots. Nutritional shortfalls, including iron, biotin and protein, or thyroid imbalances, can further disrupt the natural growth cycle and thin hair over time.

Sudden and medical hair loss

Sudden loss is a different journey altogether. Chemotherapy induced hair loss can occur almost overnight, as treatment targets healthy follicles alongside everything else, sometimes causing total loss of scalp and body hair within a very short time.

This type of loss is usually temporary, but the physical change can feel overwhelming and the emotional adjustment profound. It calls for solutions that are both sensitive and genuinely effective, and for support that treats the person, not just the hair.

What causes female hair loss?+

Genetics, hormonal changes, stress, nutritional deficiencies, autoimmune conditions or medical treatments. Common triggers include pregnancy, menopause, PCOS, thyroid disorders and rapid weight loss. Identifying the cause helps determine whether loss is temporary or progressive.

Can stress really cause hair loss?+

Yes. Stress can trigger telogen effluvium, where follicles enter a resting phase and shed prematurely, often appearing a few months after a stressful event or illness. This type of loss is usually temporary once stress levels stabilise.

What is the difference between genetic and medical hair loss?+

Genetic, or female pattern, loss develops slowly, showing as thinning at the crown or parting, and is usually permanent. Medical loss, from chemotherapy or conditions like alopecia areata, can occur suddenly and may reverse depending on the condition.

At what age does female hair loss usually start?+

At any age, though many women first notice thinning in their 30s or 40s. Hormonal changes, pregnancy and menopause are common triggers; younger women can experience loss through stress, diet or autoimmune factors.

Does hair loss from pregnancy or menopause grow back?+

Post pregnancy shedding is common and usually resolves within a year. Menopausal loss tends to be gradual and ongoing as oestrogen falls, reducing density and fineness. Early support helps minimise long term thinning.

When should I see a doctor about hair loss?+

If loss is sudden, patchy, or accompanied by other symptoms such as fatigue or hormonal changes. A GP or dermatologist can run tests, identify underlying causes and recommend medical treatment where appropriate.
